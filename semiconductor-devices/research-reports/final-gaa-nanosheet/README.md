# Final Report - Gate-All-Around (GAA) Nanosheet Field-Effect Transistors

**Course:** Advanced Electronic and Semiconductor Device Laboratory (ECEN 4002)  
**Date:** Fall 2025  

## Overview

This report presents a comprehensive technical analysis of Gate-All-Around
(GAA) nanosheet field-effect transistors, a leading device architecture for
CMOS scaling beyond FinFET technology at sub-5 nm technology nodes.

The paper examines device structure, electrostatic behavior, scaling trends,
and circuit-level performance, and compares nanosheet FETs to alternative
architectures such as TreeFETs and CombFETs.

The full report is available in `LeoMarcinkusFinalReport-4002.pdf`

## Technical Objectives

- Analyze GAA nanosheet transistor structure and operation
- Evaluate electrostatic control and short-channel suppression
- Study scaling behavior with nanosheet width and stack count
- Compare nanosheet FETs with competing architectures
- Assess circuit-level performance implications
- Investigate future CMOS scaling pathways

## Methodology

The report combines literature review, analytical modeling, and simulation
results to evaluate nanosheet FET performance trends.

Methods include:
- Comparative analysis of published experimental data
- Review of simulated transfer characteristics
- Analytical scaling evaluation
- Circuit-level performance assessment
- Technology roadmap analysis

## Key Findings

- Full gate enclosure significantly improves electrostatic control
- Vertically stacked channels increase effective width without area penalty
- Drive current scales nearly linearly with stack count
- Nanosheet FETs outperform TreeFETs and CombFETs in energy efficiency
- Device geometry tuning enables application-specific optimization

## Results and Impact

The analysis demonstrates that GAA nanosheet FETs provide the most scalable
path forward for continued CMOS performance and energy-efficiency
improvements at advanced technology nodes.

These devices enable:
- High-performance logic at reduced supply voltage
- Improved leakage control
- Enhanced transistor density
- Continued Moore’s Law scaling

## Lessons Learned

- Electrostatics dominate performance at nanoscale dimensions
- Vertical integration is critical for continued scaling
- Device-level decisions strongly affect circuit behavior
- Tradeoffs exist between manufacturability and performance
- Advanced device architectures require co-optimization
